## Narrative

### Scouting — where does the copy live, and does the repo already have it?
The session opened by mapping the two ends of the problem at once. A glob over `app/services/**` returned nine job-site PNGs (`electrical`, `painting`, `plumbing`, `carpentry`, `flooring`, `gardening`, `handyman`, `make-safe`, `preventive`). Greps for `modal` / `Modal` and `Enquir` / `enquir` converged on the two files that matter: `components/apmg/ServicesPortal.tsx` (the card grid and the `SERVICES` array) and `components/apmg/ServiceInquiryModal.tsx` (the dialog itself).

A `WebFetch` of the live `/services` page returned **10 service cards**, each with a short blurb and a **"Tell me more"** link. A second fetch established that "Tell me more" is not an on-page modal at all — it's a link to a detail page (`/services/electrical`, `/services/plumbing`, `/services/carpentry-joinery`, and so on). So the copy the user wanted was one hop further out than the request implied.

Before scraping, the obvious question: does the repo already hold this copy? `lib/ai/portalChat.ts` documents that the portal chat bubble is grounded on `components/knowledgebase/business.md` (loaded via `lib/pipeline/sectorStore.ts`), so the knowledge base was the natural candidate. Greps into `components/knowledgebase` found only trade *names* in a list (`- Carpentry & Joinery`, `- Flooring`, `- Electrical`, `- Plumbing`, `- Gardening`…), not the descriptions. The copy genuinely had to come off the site.

### The copy-fidelity re-check, and the Gardening miss
The review pass pushed back on trusting an LLM-summarised fetch as a *verbatim* source. So all 8 detail pages were re-downloaded as **raw HTML** with `curl` (80KB–129KB each) and the prose extracted locally. `python3` turned out to be the Windows App-Execution-Alias stub (`Python was not found; run without arguments to install from the Microsoft Store`), so extraction fell back to an inline `node -e` script.

That is how the real miss surfaced — **two** defects in one description. The workflow's **Gardening** copy had dropped the entire second paragraph *and* paraphrased the closing sentence of the first ("Your grounds stay safe, tidy, and looking their best all year round." for the site's "Our work keeps your grounds safe, tidy, and looking their best all year round."). Both were rewritten against the raw page, restoring the "Our team delivers precision and care on every job…" paragraph. Seven further edits then replaced straight `'` with the site's curly `’` (Electrical, Painting, Plumbing, Carpentry, Flooring, Handyman, Make Safe) so the shipped strings match the published copy character-for-character — the review had named only Plumbing and Make Safe; the sweep covered all seven descriptions that contained one.

Gardening's **bullet list** was self-flagged too (not by the review) — the flattened page text showed a line reading "Our Grounds Maintenance services cover a wide range in Melbourne, including", while the raw HTML also contains a *numbered* list (`"listStyle":"numbered"`) whose items are "Consistent and reliable maintenance", "Skilled team with attention to detail", "Flexible schedules tailored to client needs"… The numbered list is what shipped, and the condensed record does not show the question being closed in-session. It has since been closed — **the shipped list is correct**, see Problems below.

One deliberate punctuation intervention is recorded in a code comment: the Handyman source reads "…or general upkeep no task is too small", missing punctuation, so an em dash was inserted. The words are otherwise untouched. (The Electrical source has a comparable missing comma — "…and emergency repairs we do it all" — and was left exactly as published.)

### The layout regression
The review's most valuable finding was structural. Adding a description panel to the right page gave that panel intrinsic height; on a short viewport (landscape phone, small laptop window) it could stretch the dialog past the screen and clip the footer's **Send** button. Four coordinated fixes:

1. The dialog itself is now capped: `flex max-h-[calc(100dvh-2rem)] overflow-hidden …` (the `2rem` accounts for the wrapper's `p-4`).
2. Header and footer got `shrink-0` so they can't be squeezed away.
3. The form became `flex min-h-0 flex-1 flex-col` and the body `max-h-[min(62vh,540px)] min-h-0 flex-1 … overflow-y-auto`, forming a flex chain where the **body** is what shrinks and scrolls.
4. The right panel became `relative … sm:block` with its contents in an `absolute inset-0 flex flex-col` — so the panel contributes **zero intrinsic height** and the form column keeps governing dialog height exactly as it did when that page was photo-only. Long copy scrolls inside its own `min-h-0 flex-1 overflow-y-auto` box.

A separate accessibility/UX bug came out of the mobile screenshots: the modal's existing Name-field autofocus scrolled the body straight past the new description (and would pop a phone keyboard over it). The reset effect now computes `descriptionLeadsForm` — true when there's a description and *not* (a photo panel at `min-width: 640px`) — and skips the autofocus in that case, leaving the focus trap's default resting place (the header Close button).

Two small tidies followed: `[max-height:min(62vh,540px)]` was rewritten as `max-h-[min(62vh,540px)]` to match the file's existing Tailwind arbitrary-value style, and the newly-wrapped right-panel block was re-indented to the file's formatting.

## Decisions
- **Verbatim, not rewritten.** The user asked for the site's descriptions, so the client's published wording is carried word-for-word, curly apostrophes included, rather than being re-voiced for the portal. This was checked against raw HTML precisely because "verbatim" was the requirement.
- **The client's copy overrides the portal's own copy rule.** `ServicesPortal.tsx` carries a long-standing comment banning superlatives from `blurb` ("flawless", "perfection" are called out by name as trust-negative with facility managers). The site's **Painting** description says "our skilled painters deliver **flawless finishes**". It was carried anyway and the tension was made explicit in the code comment, which now separates the portal-authored `blurb` (rule applies) from the client-published `description`/`includes` (verbatim wins). Flagged to the user rather than silently softened.
- **Hard-code the copy; don't fetch it at runtime and don't route it through the knowledge base.** A runtime fetch of a third-party marketing site would put an external dependency in the enquiry path. The sync is manual and dated in a comment instead — with the "edit the site, not this file" instruction to keep the site as the source of truth.
- **One `ServiceDescription`, two hosts.** The desktop book panel and the mobile in-form block share a single renderer specifically so the two surfaces can never drift apart.
- **The right panel must contribute zero intrinsic height.** `absolute inset-0` keeps the form column the sole governor of dialog height, which is what made the pre-existing short-viewport behaviour correct; long copy scrolls inside the panel instead of growing the dialog.
- **Skip the autofocus rather than fight it.** When the description leads the form body, the focus trap's default (Close button) is the correct resting place — the alternative was scrolling the visitor past the exact copy the change exists to surface.
- **Only the 8 services that have portal cards were populated.** The site has 10 service pages; **Reactive** and **Preventative** have no card in the portal, so no copy was added for them. Their copy was fetched in-session but only ever lived in the workflow task output under the temp store — it is **not** in the repo. It has not been lost, though: `…/379a7ed4…/tasks/wax6mmvha.output` still held both pages' intro paragraph and bullets on 2026-07-30. Their live URLs are `/services/reactive-services` and `/services/preventative-services` (`/services/reactive` and `/services/preventative` both 404). The temp store is purgeable, so treat that file as a convenience copy, not a source of truth.

## Problems / gotchas
- **An LLM-summarised fetch is not a verbatim source.** The background fetch workflow silently dropped the **Gardening** description's whole second paragraph *and* paraphrased the closing sentence of its first. It was only caught because the review pass forced a raw-HTML re-check of all 8 pages. If the same pattern was used for any other page's copy without a raw check, treat it as suspect. (The other 7 descriptions came through the raw-HTML diff word-for-word — only their apostrophes needed normalising — and a post-session re-check confirmed all 8; see below.)
- **The Gardening "What's included" list looked wrong but is right.** What shipped is the detail page's *numbered* list — "Consistent and reliable maintenance", "Skilled team with attention to detail", "Flexible schedules tailored to client needs", "Safe and compliant grounds management", "Proven experience with large outdoor spaces" — which reads as a why-choose-us list, not a list of works, and the same page carries the line "Our Grounds Maintenance services cover a wide range in Melbourne, including", which looks like it should introduce a *different* list. **Resolved 2026-07-30 against the live page:** that line is the subheading of the very "What We Can Do" section the numbered list sits in (heading, subheading, `"listPosition":"right"` list — one block), the page contains no other list, and this is the identical structural slot every other service page uses for its includes list (Electrical's subheading reads "Our Licensed Electrical services cover a wide range of needs, including:" above its six-item list). The why-choose-us flavour is the client's own page content, not a scraping error. Nothing to fix in the repo; if anyone wants a genuine list of gardening works, the request goes to the client's site.
- **Copy fidelity re-verified after the session.** On 2026-07-30 all 8 detail pages were re-downloaded and are **byte-identical** to the session's captures, and every shipped `description` paragraph and `includes` item was found verbatim in its page's HTML — 8/8, with the single deliberate Handyman em dash as the only deviation. - **The session's closing summary contained one inaccuracy worth correcting:** it described `app/services/preventive.png` as "unused".
